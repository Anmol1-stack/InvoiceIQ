PAYMENT POLICY

Invoices must be paid within thirty days from the invoice date unless different payment terms have been agreed in writing.

The payment due date is calculated from the invoice date. For example, an invoice dated June 1 is due on July 1.

Customers should make payments using one of the payment methods specified by the service provider.

When making a payment, customers should include the invoice number or payment reference so that the payment can be matched to the correct invoice.

Late payments may be subject to applicable interest charges.

The service provider may send a payment reminder when an invoice becomes overdue.

If a customer expects difficulty making a payment before the due date, the customer should contact the service provider as soon as possible.

A payment reminder does not change the original payment deadline unless a different arrangement is confirmed in writing.

The customer must notify the service provider of any disputed invoice within ten business days.

A dispute should identify the invoice number, the amount being disputed, and the reason for the dispute.

Submitting a dispute does not automatically suspend payment of the entire invoice.

Undisputed portions of an invoice remain payable even when another portion is disputed.

If a customer pays an invoice after the due date, applicable late payment charges may be added according to the agreed terms.

Customers should retain payment confirmations and transaction references for their records.

If a payment has been made but is not reflected on the account, the customer should provide the payment reference to the service provider for investigation.
