CONTRACT POLICY

Either party may request changes to the agreement, but amendments must be agreed to in writing by the authorized representatives of both parties.

Contract amendments should clearly identify the sections being changed and the effective date of the amendment.

An amendment does not automatically change unrelated provisions of the agreement.

Either party may terminate the agreement by providing thirty days written notice unless a different termination period has been agreed in the contract.

Immediate termination may occur when a party materially breaches the agreement and fails to correct the breach within fifteen days after receiving written notice.

Termination does not remove obligations relating to confidentiality, intellectual property, or payments that became due before termination.

Outstanding payment obligations remain applicable after termination.

Contract renewals should be reviewed before the current agreement expires.

Any renewal terms should be documented in writing and approved by the appropriate representatives.

The parties should review important contractual terms before agreeing to a renewal.

Confidentiality obligations may continue after the agreement ends when specified in the contract.

Intellectual property rights and obligations should be handled according to the terms of the applicable agreement.

Changes to payment terms should be documented in writing.

A verbal discussion about changing contract terms does not by itself replace the written agreement unless the contract expressly allows such changes.

Each party should retain copies of the current agreement and approved amendments for reference.
