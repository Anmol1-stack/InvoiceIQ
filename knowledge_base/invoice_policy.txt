INVOICE POLICY

All invoices must contain the invoice number, invoice date, supplier name, customer name, description of goods or services, applicable taxes, total amount, payment terms, and payment instructions.

Each invoice should have a unique invoice number.

The invoice date should accurately represent the date on which the invoice was issued.

Invoices should clearly identify the goods or services for which the customer is being charged.

The total amount should reflect the applicable charges, discounts, taxes, and adjustments.

Invoices should be sent to the designated billing contact of the customer.

Customers are responsible for providing accurate billing information and notifying the service provider when billing details change.

If an invoice contains an incorrect amount, tax value, customer detail, or description, the customer should contact the service provider.

A corrected invoice may be issued when an error is identified.

The original invoice should remain available for audit and record-keeping purposes when a corrected invoice is issued.

Duplicate invoice numbers should not be used for different transactions.

Electronic invoices are considered valid when they contain the required information and can be retained for record-keeping purposes.

Customers should review invoices after receiving them and report suspected errors promptly.

An invoice correction does not automatically extend the payment deadline unless the service provider confirms a revised deadline.

Invoices should be retained according to applicable organizational record-keeping requirements.
