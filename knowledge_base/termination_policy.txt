TERMINATION POLICY

Either party may terminate the agreement by providing thirty days written notice unless a different termination period has been agreed in writing.

Termination notices should clearly identify the agreement being terminated and the intended termination date.

Immediate termination may occur if a party materially breaches the agreement and fails to correct the breach within fifteen days after receiving written notice.

A material breach may include a significant failure to perform an important contractual obligation.

The party receiving a breach notice should take appropriate corrective action within the specified fifteen-day period.

If the breach is corrected within the allowed period, immediate termination on the basis of that breach may not occur.

Termination does not remove obligations relating to confidentiality, intellectual property, or payments that became due before termination.

Outstanding invoices remain payable even after the agreement has been terminated.

Termination of a contract does not automatically cancel amounts that were already owed by either party.

Any termination-related communication should be provided in writing through an authorized communication channel.

Customers should retain a copy of the termination notice and any confirmation received from the service provider.

Where the agreement specifies a different notice period, the terms of that agreement take precedence.

The parties should complete any required final payments, documentation, or handover activities following termination.

Confidentiality obligations that survive termination continue according to the applicable agreement.
