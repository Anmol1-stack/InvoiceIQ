DISPUTE POLICY

Customers must notify the service provider of any disputed invoice within ten business days of receiving the invoice.

A dispute should identify the invoice number, the disputed amount, the reason for the dispute, and any supporting documentation.

Examples of disputed items may include an incorrect charge, incorrect quantity, incorrect tax amount, duplicate charge, or service that was not received.

The service provider will review the disputed information and may request additional documentation from the customer.

Submitting a dispute does not automatically suspend payment of the entire invoice.

Undisputed portions of an invoice remain payable even when another portion is disputed.

Customers should continue to pay any undisputed amount according to the original payment terms.

The service provider may contact the customer during the investigation to clarify the disputed transaction.

If the dispute is found to be valid, the service provider may issue a corrected invoice, credit, refund, or other appropriate adjustment.

If the dispute is found to be invalid, the original invoice amount may remain payable.

Customers should retain copies of documents and communications submitted during the dispute process.

A dispute should be raised as soon as the customer identifies an issue rather than waiting until the payment deadline.

The outcome of a dispute should be communicated to the customer after the review has been completed.

If a customer disagrees with the outcome, the customer may provide additional supporting information for further review.
